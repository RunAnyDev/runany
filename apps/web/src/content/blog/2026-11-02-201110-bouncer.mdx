> **TL;DR:** Bouncer reads your X feed through a small language model, hides the posts you want gone, and teaches the algorithm to send fewer of them. It ships as a Chrome extension, a Firefox add-on and a free iPhone app, and an experimental mode keeps the filtering on your own machine. Everything so far works on X only.

## What it is

Bouncer is a feed filter for X, built by Imbue, the research lab behind Imbue Studio. You describe what you want removed in plain language, it scores every post in your timeline against those rules, and it hides the ones that match.

The part that separates it from a keyword blocker is that the filter reads context and tone rather than matching exact strings. You can write "arrogance" or "pessimism" and it works out what that means for a given post. The second half of the trick is feedback into the ranking: when Bouncer hides a post, X sees no engagement on it, and the feed shifts over the following hours.

It ships in three places, a Chrome extension, a Firefox add-on and an iPhone app on the App Store. The Chrome listing was on version 2.8.4, updated 23 October 2026, with 9,000 users and a 4.6 rating from 46 ratings at the time of writing. All three are free, and the iPhone app lists at $0.

## Why it matters

The ranker is not working for you. It is working for engagement, and whatever produces a reaction gets promoted, which is why a feed that used to be a source of real-time local information turns into a stream of posts engineered to make you angry.

Imbue's own framing is blunt about the trade-off. Millan Philipose wrote that he stopped opening the For You tab entirely and felt better for it, but also lost the thing that made X worth keeping for him, which was finding people through local politics coverage. Quitting works. It is also the only option most keyword blockers offer.

Bouncer sits in between. You stay on the platform, but you get a say in the ranking, and the filters are semantic enough to express the thing you actually mind. If a post annoys you because of how it is written rather than which words it contains, that is the difference it makes.

## What it filters

Four mechanisms do the work, and the extension surfaces all of them in a panel that sits to the right of the timeline.

| Mechanism | What it does |
|---|---|
| Filter by phrase | Applies a rule you write to every post, including open-ended ones like "arrogance" or even the color "green" |
| AI slop removal | A separate model flags AI-generated text and images, switched on with the star icon or by asking to filter out "AI slop" |
| Bounce this post | The bin icon on any tweet gives you three reasons you might find it annoying, and picking one removes that post and similar ones |
| View filtered | Every removed post is kept with a justification, and you can restore or re-filter an individual one with a click |

The phrase filters are worth playing with, because the examples in the Chrome listing are deliberately loose:

```text
arrogance
pessimism
text-only tweets
green
AI slop
```

None of those are keywords to block. They are descriptions of a mood, and the model decides per post whether the description applies.

## Where the model runs

This is the part that matters if you care where your timeline goes, and Imbue is clear about the split.

By default the classification happens on Imbue's own servers, using a mixture-of-experts model they describe as Gemma 4 26B-A4B. Twenty-six billion parameters total with four billion active is what lets it keep up with scroll speed while still reading context.

There is an on-device mode, flagged experimental in settings, that runs the filtering locally instead. The Chrome listing says on-device mode uses Gemma 4 E2B. In a reply on the launch thread the team said they are forking WebLLM, adding support for more modern multimodal models and optimising so an M4 chip can keep up with scrolling. On iPhone they are still working on getting 4B models onto the Apple Neural Engine, and they named battery life as the main bottleneck.

| Mode | Model | Where the filtering runs |
|---|---|---|
| Default | Gemma 4 26B-A4B | Imbue data center |
| On-device (experimental) | Gemma 4 E2B | Your own machine |

Worth being precise about the privacy claim. The Chrome listing states that Bouncer does not collect or store any identifying data, and that reads as true of the extension itself. In the default mode your post content still travels to Imbue's servers to be classified, which is exactly what the local mode exists to avoid.

## Getting it running

All three installs are store pages, so there is no package manager involved. On macOS you can hand them to the browser:

```bash
# Chrome extension
open "https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/bouncer-filter-your-feed/bkijmhafoocfloemhancbgadknkgdkcm"

# Firefox add-on
open "https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/bouncer-heal-your-feed"

# iPhone app, opens the App Store
open "macappstores://itunes.apple.com/app/id6759466393"
```

On Linux, swap `open` for `xdg-open`. The links themselves are stable enough to keep:

```text
Chrome extension ID   bkijmhafoocfloemhancbgadknkgdkcm
Chrome Web Store      https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/bouncer-filter-your-feed/bkijmhafoocfloemhancbgadknkgdkcm
Firefox add-on        https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/bouncer-heal-your-feed
App Store             https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bouncer-heal-your-feed/id6759466393
```

The extension ID matters if you manage Chrome by policy, since it lets you allow or block the extension in a managed browser rather than per machine.

Once it is installed, open the panel next to the timeline and add one rule. Resist the urge to write ten. Bounce a few posts first with the bin icon, because the three reasons it offers are usually more precise than anything you would have written, and each bounced post seeds a rule for you.

## What to watch for

X only, for now. Reddit and LinkedIn feeds are on the roadmap along with Safari support, so nothing here applies to the other places you read. The extension is also English only, about 6.95 MiB, and there is no published pricing beyond the free store listings.

The healing effect is a bet on the ranking system rather than a guarantee. The Chrome listing claims the algorithm adapts within hours, and the mechanism is that X observes a lower engagement signal on posts Bouncer hides. If you bounce aggressively in the first hour, your feed may look emptier than it did before, not better.

The model names are vendor claims. Both the 26B-A4B and the E2B naming comes from Imbue, and the on-device path is described as experimental rather than finished. Nobody outside the company has benchmarked either, and the local mode on a phone is explicitly a work in progress.

Bouncer hides posts by default, which means you only see what it removed if you open View filtered. That is a deliberate choice, but it also means a badly worded rule can quietly swallow things you wanted to read. The filtered list lets you recover any single post, and it is worth checking it every so often rather than trusting the filter blindly.

Finally, there is no source code for Bouncer itself. The models are open-source per Imbue, and the company is a trading entity in San Francisco, Imbue, Inc., which is also the developer name on the Chrome listing.
