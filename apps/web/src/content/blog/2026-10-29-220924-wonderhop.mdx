> **TL;DR:** Wonderhop is a free daily guessing game you play in a browser, where the goal is to type the answer most people give (Sky) or a real answer almost nobody gives (Abyss). Four short games a day, no account, no ads, and the questions reset at midnight in Montreal so everyone plays the same set.

## What it is

Wonderhop is a daily survey-style guessing game from two independent developers in Montreal, launched on 5 October 2026. Every player gets the same four games of seven questions each day, and each question asks you to name something, like "Name a food many people hate". You type a real answer and the game tells you how common it is compared to what other people submitted.

The twist is that it ships in four flavours rather than one. The same question is asked four ways, so the identical question can be a popularity contest in one mode and a knowledge hunt in another. The questions roll over at midnight Montreal time, which keeps every player on the same set and makes the daily leaderboard meaningful.

The visuals are retro pixel art with a chiptune soundtrack, and they change per mode. The rabbit mascot rides a hot-air balloon upward in Sky and sinks in a diving suit in Abyss, so your score is visible as altitude rather than a number. The front end is a static Vite-built React bundle, and the site ships a web app manifest, so you can add it to the home screen on a phone and launch it like a native app.

## The four modes

| Mode | What you are hunting for | Timer per question |
|---|---|---|
| Sky | the answer most people give | 30 seconds |
| Abyss | a real answer almost nobody gives | 40 seconds |
| Past | the oldest answer to the question | 30 seconds |
| Future | the newest answer to the question | 30 seconds |

Sky works like a Family Feud board where you are alone with the answer sheet, and the more popular your answer is, the higher the balloon climbs. Abyss inverts it: the rarer your answer, the deeper the rabbit sinks, and it gets a longer clock because the space of valid answers is thin. Past and Future add a time axis, comparing your answer against every other answer by year, so each good guess sends the rabbit centuries forward or backward.

Your answers are typed freely rather than picked from a list, and they are judged against a fixed answer list first. Anything that misses that list goes to an AI judge, which explains why it rejected your answer instead of silently failing.

## Scoring

Each question is worth up to 100 points, so a full game tops out at 700 and a full day at 2,800. Grades run from Perfect in gold down through Excellent, Great, OK and Weak, and what counts as Perfect depends on the mode:

| Mode | What Perfect requires |
|---|---|
| Sky | the single number one answer on the list |
| Abyss | rarer than 97% of what other players answered |
| Past | among the oldest 3% by year |
| Future | among the newest 3% by year |

In Past and Future the year of each answer is what counts, and those years come from Wikipedia, which has its own idea of when something first appeared. A robot toy created in 2015 counts as 2015 even if its viral moment came years later, and a palace whose construction started in the fifteenth century counts from then, not from its modern reopening.

The game runs in nine languages, and answers are accepted in whichever one you are playing in:

- English
- French
- Spanish
- Portuguese
- German
- Italian
- Dutch
- Polish
- Turkish

## Playing it

You can play immediately at wonderhop.io with no signup. If you want to appear on the leaderboard you pick a nickname, otherwise you just start. There is also an itch.io listing where the same game is marked as free, HTML5, and flagged as AI assisted for its text.

To put the game on your own page, itch.io publishes this embed snippet:


## What to watch for

The judge is the part worth testing before you settle in. The developer explains that most answers are checked against a fixed list first, specifically to avoid making too many LLM calls, and puts the error rate at roughly 1 to 1.5% of answers ending up judged unfairly. Misspellings are the usual casualty, and players reported valid answers being refused for spelling reasons. The judge has also been gamed: someone found an LLM loophole that accepted a deliberately oblique answer, and the developer said they would close it.

No account means no cross-device progress. Your game state lives in the browser you played on, so a nickname is the only thing that carries over, and only to the leaderboard.

The free tier is the daily set only. The optional Hop Pass is 9.99 USD once or 3 USD per month, and it unlocks unlimited play, a thirty-day archive of past days, and every theme. The archive is the part that actually matters if Past and Future are your modes, since those games only make sense against a pool of answers you can revisit.

Mobile had real bugs in the first version, with the on-screen keyboard covering the answer box on iOS Safari and the question scrolling off screen on Firefox Android. The developer says both are fixed, but it costs one minute to check on your own device.
