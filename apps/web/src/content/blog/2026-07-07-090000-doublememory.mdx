## What Is DoubleMemory?

DoubleMemory is a macOS and iOS app that sits in your menu bar or dock and automatically captures anything you double-tap Cmd+C. Instead of flooding your clipboard history with every copy, it silently saves links and text after detecting a second rapid copy — then renders them as Pinterest-style cards you can search, tag, and browse offline.

The pitch from the HN launch: *"I wanted to see if I could automatically save all double cmd+c without needing extensions, and avoid flooding the clipboard history like regular clipboard managers do."*

The product evolved over six months into a read-it-later + bookmarking organizer + clipboard manager + card-based note-taking app — all local-first, all private.

## Setup Workflow

### Step 1: Download and Launch

Download DoubleMemory from the Mac App Store or directly from [doublememory.com](https://doublememory.com). The app launches into your menu bar (macOS) or as a homescreen app (iOS). No sign-up, no email, no account creation screen.

### Step 2: Grant Clipboard Monitoring Permission

On first launch, macOS will prompt you to grant clipboard monitoring access. This allows DoubleMemory to detect when you double-tap Cmd+C — it does **not** read your clipboard continuously, only the second tap within a short window. The privacy policy states explicitly that captured content stays on-device and is never sent to DoubleMemory's servers.

### Step 3: Start Capturing

From any app, just double-tap Cmd+C. DoubleMemory saves the link or text as a card. You can also:

- Use the **Share extension** from any macOS or iOS app
- **Drag and drop** content onto the Dock icon or menu bar icon
- Import your existing Safari Reading List and Bookmarks via the built-in importer tool

### Step 4: Sync with iCloud

Open DoubleMemory settings and sign in with your Apple ID to enable iCloud sync. Your cards travel between your Mac and iOS devices automatically. The sync uses Apple's end-to-end encryption — DoubleMemory as a company never sees your data.

## Key Features

### Automatic Capture via Double Cmd+C

The core mechanic: most clipboard managers record every single copy. DoubleMemory only captures on a double-tap, which better matches how people actually save links instinctively.

### On-Device Apple Intelligence Auto-Tagging

Screenshots are automatically OCR'd and tagged using Apple's on-device AI. Tags like `#screenshot`, `#design`, `#inspiration` are generated without any cloud processing.

### Local-First Architecture

All saved content lives on your Mac or iPhone. iCloud sync makes it available across devices, but the primary store is always local — meaning DoubleMemory works fully offline.

### Reader Mode and Beautiful Preview Cards

Saved links render as uniform preview cards. Toggle Reader Mode for distraction-free text. Cards are laid out on a Pinterest-style mood board rather than a plain list.

### Import from Pocket, Readwise, Omnivore, MyMind

The importer page at [doublememory.com/posts/import](https://doublememory.com/posts/import) provides browser-based tools to migrate from most major read-it-later services.

### Privacy by Design

From the site: *"DoubleMemory does not store your saved content on its own servers. Your data stays on your Mac/iPhone/iCloud."* The app is "default alive" — it works without internet and without the company's servers.

## FAQ

**Q: How is this different from a clipboard manager?**
**A:** Regular clipboard managers log every single copy — DoubleMemory only captures a double-tap, which is a more intentional signal that you want to save something. It also presents saves as browsable cards rather than a chronological clipboard log.

**Q: Is my data really private?**
**A:** According to the product's own privacy documentation, captured content is stored locally on your device and synced through your personal iCloud account. DoubleMemory as a company states they do not store your saved content on their servers.

**Q: Does it work without internet?**
**A:** Yes. Core capture, search, and organization work completely offline. iCloud sync requires internet to propagate changes between devices.

**Q: Is there a Windows or Android version?**
**A:** Currently macOS and iOS only. The team has stated they want to capture a small portion of the Apple market before expanding.
