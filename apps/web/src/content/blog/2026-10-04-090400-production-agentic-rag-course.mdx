## Orchestration is the differentiator

The `airflow` directory is the part most RAG tutorials skip. A retrieval pipeline that runs once from a notebook teaches retrieval. A pipeline scheduled by Airflow teaches what production actually asks of you: dependencies between stages, retries, backfills, and the ability to look at what ran yesterday.

Docker Compose and a Dockerfile alongside it mean the supporting services are meant to be started as a set rather than installed by hand, and the Gradio launcher gives you a UI over the result. A Makefile sits on top of that for the repetitive commands.

## When to use it

Good fit if you have built a RAG prototype from a notebook and hit the wall where nothing runs on a schedule, nothing is retried, and you cannot tell what happened overnight. This repository is an answer to that specific problem, and the Airflow layer is the part you are unlikely to get from a blog post.

It is also a reasonable skeleton to fork. MIT means you can drop it into your own project and swap the retrieval logic for your own, keeping the orchestration you would otherwise have to write.

## When to skip it

Skip it if your goal is evaluating retrieval quality. The system is built around pipeline mechanics, so you will spend your time on Airflow and Docker rather than on chunking and ranking.

Mind the four-month gap if you plan to follow along with current library versions. Expect to spend time on dependency conflicts before you get to the actual material, and pin your environment early.
