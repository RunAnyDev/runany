## What Is TeardownHQ?

TeardownHQ is an intelligence platform for SaaS founders and analysts. Its homepage displays a live dashboard tracking over 1,742 startups with estimated sourced metrics — aggregated MRR, 30-day growth rates, and weekly new additions. All figures are labeled as estimates where they are estimates.

The core product is the teardown: a deep-dive report on a specific SaaS company that covers the growth channels it used, its pricing history, and its go-to-market strategy — sourced and dated, not self-reported.

The HN launch post describes it as "teardowns/playbooks of exactly how each one grew. The channels, the pricing, the GTM, all sourced, instead of the usual self-reported screenshots."

## Key Features

**1. Startup Directory**

A searchable, browsable directory of tracked SaaS companies. Each entry shows estimated ARR, growth trajectory, category, and founding date. The platform currently shows $18.4M in aggregate tracked MRR across its database.

**2. Deep Teardowns**

Detailed reports on individual companies. Example from the homepage: "How AIWriteBook turned a fragmented book-publishing stack into one $12 subscription." Each teardown is manually researched and sourced — not community-contributed.

Teardowns cover:
- The specific channels used to acquire customers
- Pricing changes over time and their effect on revenue
- GTM strategy and positioning shifts
- Actual growth numbers (not vanity metrics)

**3. Market Pulse**

Aggregate views across the tracked portfolio: MRR trends over time, growth rate distributions, and category-level benchmarks. This gives individual founders context for how their metrics compare to the broader indie SaaS landscape.

**4. Directory Browse**

The directory can be filtered by category, making it useful for competitive research within a specific niche. Categories visible on the site include Analytics, Content Creation, Design Tools, SaaS, Fintech, and Games.

## How Teardowns Are Sourced

The TeardownHQ team researches each company manually — pulling public data from job postings, public revenue signals, pricing pages archived over time, and operator interviews. Where a metric is an estimate, the site labels it as such.

This is in contrast to most "X made $Y in 30 days" content, which relies on self-reported, cherry-picked screenshots. The TeardownHQ approach tries to triangulate real figures and document the methodology.

## Pricing

TeardownHQ operates on a subscription model. Custom teardowns are available as a separate paid service. A free account provides limited access to some teardowns — the homepage notes "free with an account" on featured teardown listings.

The specific pricing tiers are available on the [pricing page](https://teardownhq.io/pricing).

## FAQ

**Q: Are the revenue figures on TeardownHQ accurate?**
**A:** TeardownHQ labels each metric as estimated or sourced. They use public signals and manual research to build estimates — they are not company-confirmed figures. Treat them as informed approximations, not audited numbers.

**Q: Is this useful for early-stage founders?**
**A:** Yes. The teardowns focus on growth channels and GTM strategy, which is most actionable at the pre-product-market-fit stage. The directory provides benchmark context for early metrics.

**Q: How is TeardownHQ different from SimilarWeb or Crunchbase?**
**A:** TeardownHQ focuses specifically on indie and bootstrapped SaaS, not enterprise or venture-backed companies at scale. Its teardowns go deeper on growth mechanics than typical directory sites, which focus on funding rounds and headcount.

**Q: Does TeardownHQ cover non-SaaS businesses?**
**A:** The platform currently focuses on SaaS companies. The directory categories (Analytics, Content Creation, Design Tools, etc.) are all SaaS-adjacent. Teardowns of non-SaaS businesses are not prominently featured.

## Conclusion

TeardownHQ fills a specific gap in the indie SaaS research space: reliable, sourced growth intelligence without the self-reported vanity bias. Its directory of 1,742 startups provides useful benchmark context, and the teardown reports offer actionable playbook-level insight into how specific companies actually grew.

If you are an indie founder trying to understand which channels work in your category — rather than which channels companies claim to have used — TeardownHQ is worth a look. The research methodology is more rigorous than most alternative sources, and the focus on bootstrapped/indie SaaS makes the data more applicable to smaller-scale operations than typical tech press coverage.
