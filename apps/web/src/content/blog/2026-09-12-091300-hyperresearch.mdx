## TL;DR

> **TL;DR:** HyperResearch is an open source research knowledge base built for AI agents. The agents collect web research, organize it into a persistent corpus, and synthesize answers from it. It is the project to reach for when you want a research surface that survives across sessions, rather than a one-off deep-research query that disappears.

## What Is HyperResearch?

The repository description: "Agent-driven research knowledge base. Agents collect, search, and synthesize web research into a persistent, searchable [corpus]." HyperResearch targets a specific failure mode of one-shot deep-research tools — they run, they return, and the next session has to redo the work.

The topics — `agents`, `agentskills`, `claude-code`, `deep-research` — confirm the integration story. HyperResearch is designed to plug into Claude Code as skills, so the agent loop you already use can write to and read from a long-lived research corpus.

## How It Works

The Python codebase ships an agent-driven research loop. The implied pipeline:

- An agent reads a research question.
- It runs web searches, fetches pages, and pulls structured data.
- It writes findings into a persistent knowledge base — local, queryable, versioned.
- It synthesizes answers from the corpus rather than redoing the search every session.

The corpus is the differentiator. Most deep-research tools end at "here is your answer." HyperResearch ends at "here is your answer, and here is the corpus you can keep querying and extending."

## When To Use It

HyperResearch is the right tool when:

- You do recurring research on a topic and want the corpus to persist across sessions.
- You use Claude Code or a similar agentic CLI that supports skills.
- You want the agent loop to own the research workflow, not a one-off deep-research call.

It is not a search engine, it is not a citation manager, and it does not replace reading the primary sources yourself. It is the persistence layer between your research and the next time you need it.
