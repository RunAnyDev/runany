## What Is ReadyBase?

ReadyBase describes itself as "the final output of any intelligence workflow" — turning AI-generated outputs, raw data, research, and insights into polished, presentation-ready documents. The core pitch is unique: every PDF or PNG generation is 100 percent unique, with no pre-built templates involved.

The product targets teams and individuals who use LLMs to research, analyze, and synthesize information but struggle to present the output in a shareable format. Instead of copying and pasting into Word or Google Docs, ReadyBase takes the raw LLM output directly and produces a formatted document.

## Setup Workflow

ReadyBase runs entirely in the browser — no installation, no self-hosting required.

### Step 1: Create a Free Account

Visit [readybase.ai](https://readybase.ai) and sign up. The pricing page shows a "Free to start, no credit card required" tier, with a "Professional reporting" tier for advanced features.

### Step 2: Connect Your AI Workflow

There is no public documentation URL in the launch thread, but the product appears to accept raw AI outputs directly. Based on the product description, you paste or pipe your LLM-generated content into ReadyBase and select your desired output format.

### Step 3: Generate Documents

Choose between PDF and PNG output. The product description mentions support for data visualization and research synthesis — these appear as structured sections within the generated document rather than flat text dumps.

ReadyBase is a proprietary SaaS tool. There is no self-hosted option, no public source repository, and no open-source license. Data processed through the service is stored on ReadyBase's infrastructure. For sensitive or proprietary data, evaluate your organization's data handling requirements before submitting content.

## Conclusion

ReadyBase solves a specific gap in the AI workflow: the "last mile" problem of turning raw LLM outputs into presentable documents. The template-free approach means each generation is genuinely unique, which differentiates it from document automation tools that rely on pre-built layouts.

If you regularly use LLMs for research, analysis, or data synthesis and spend significant time reformatting outputs into shareable documents, ReadyBase is worth trying. The free tier gives you enough to evaluate whether the workflow fits your process.
