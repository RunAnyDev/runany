> **TL;DR:** Foolscap is a browser writing app for book-length work that reads your manuscript as you type, then leaves threads about what connects and what hangs loose, without ever putting a word into your text. Plans run from free to EUR 29 a month, and every book can be switched to a private mode where nothing reaches an AI provider at all.

## What Foolscap actually does

Foolscap is a web app at foolscap.works, built by a developer named David who shipped his first public product in November 2026. The pitch on the home page is short enough to quote: "A writing app that knows your whole book. As you write, it notices what connects and what's left hanging, and shows you where, in your own words. It never writes a word for you."

The mechanics matter more than the pitch. Foolscap sits alongside a manuscript in the browser, a manuscript editor with a contents sidebar on the left, chapter headings, and a Threads panel on the right. As you write, it reads along and leaves notes in that panel. The notes are not generic suggestions. In the vendor's own demo video, a thread on a draft novel titled "Book" picks out a passage where the narrator describes the sky and asks whether the writer meant "the light pass out of me" or "the clouds are on fire". A second thread, headed "Restatements that stall", points at a repeated beat in the prose and offers a rewritten version of one line.

Each thread can be accepted or rejected, and a rejected thread can still be opened for a conversation about why.

![Foolscap's writing page with a fiction manuscript in the centre and a Threads panel on the right listing open questions about the manuscript](https://cdn.runany.dev/blog/inline/foolscap-ui.webp)

What Foolscap never does is type into your book. Threads are offered, and the writer decides.

## The bet: AI as an exoskeleton

David's motivation is spelled out in his Show HN post, and it explains the design choices better than any feature list could.

He writes that he began building Foolscap because of how the maths community reacted to LLMs solving open problems. The dread was the point for him, because much of the value of doing maths comes from the struggle. His question was whether the same technology could be turned around to make the hard work of thinking more rewarding and deeper rather than obsolete.

In conversation on that thread he calls the approach an "exoskeleton": amplifying your thinking rather than replacing it. The community response was broadly warm, with a few pointed questions. One commenter noted that Fleshnote, a separate pre-release writing tool, works from a similar write-first, note-second approach and calls its revision feature "git for prose". David's answer was that Foolscap keeps each book's history in git too, and that everything should transparently belong to the user.

That last line turns out to be the real product decision.

## How it reads and how it links

Two mechanisms carry most of the value.

The first is the reader. Foolscap watches the manuscript and pulls out structure: characters, ideas, threads, anything left hanging. It does this in the writer's own vocabulary rather than in the vocabulary of a writing coach, which is why the demo threads read like a careful editor's marginalia instead of a prompt template.

The second is colour linking. Passages in the manuscript are highlighted, and the same colour appears on the thread in the side panel that refers to them. Clicking the thread takes you to the passage. It is a small visual trick, and it is the difference between a list of suggestions and a map of your own book.

![The same manuscript with passages highlighted and colour-linked to threads in the right-hand panel](https://cdn.runany.dev/blog/inline/foolscap-colour.webp)

Behind that sits a side agent plugged into the same index. You can ask it about the book and fire off actions through conversation, and because it reads the same structure the reader does, its answers are anchored to your manuscript rather than to a generic model of novels.

There is dictation too, billed separately from the reading quota, and a maths mode that the vendor led with in the demo: "Every book, maths and all."

## The maths mode is the surprise

The demo video spends its final third inside a manuscript that is not prose at all. It is a mathematical text with a section called "The central limit theorem", rendered as real LaTeX, with the characteristic-function proof laid out and the notation typeset properly rather than approximated.

![A mathematics manuscript showing the central limit theorem rendered in LaTeX, with a research thread citing Wikipedia, Encyclopedia of Mathematics and ScienceDirect](https://cdn.runany.dev/blog/inline/foolscap-maths.webp)

The thread panel on that screen is doing something worth stealing for any research-heavy writing. It holds a claim, stated in the writer's own words: the proof works with characteristic functions and does not need the moment method. Underneath sit the actual citations, links to Wikipedia, to the Encyclopedia of Mathematics, and to a ScienceDirect entry on the central limit theorem, along with a note that the proof can be derived from the Möbius-Laplace theorem. The system flags the methodological question and points to where it is written down.

In other words, the app can follow a claim through a book, notice that the argument depends on something you may not have established, and then go and look it up.

David is careful about how much of this is finished. In the launch post he writes that the system can follow theorems and proofs and string together their dependencies, but that he has not made that a focus yet. There is no Lean support, and he asked the thread for feedback on it. So treat the maths side as a real capability that is still early, not a finished product.

## Getting it running

There is nothing to install. Foolscap is a browser app at write.foolscap.works behind a sign-in, and the site sends its own noindex header for the app itself, so you will not find it by searching for the tool name.

If you want to look before you sign up, the vendor's demo video is the fastest route, and it is worth pulling out yourself if you would rather see the interface at full size than inside a browser page.

```bash
curl -sL -A "Mozilla/5.0" "https://www.foolscap.works/media/foolscap-phone.webm?v=6" -o /tmp/foolscap-demo.webm
ffprobe -v error -show_entries format=duration,size -of default=noprint_wrappers=1 /tmp/foolscap-demo.webm
ffmpeg -v error -ss 6 -i /tmp/foolscap-demo.webm -frames:v 1 -y /tmp/frame.png
```

That gives you a 1000x478 frame of the real writing page. The video runs about nineteen seconds, so stepping past the eighteen second mark lands on the maths manuscript instead.

Plans are priced in euros and can be paid monthly or yearly.

| Plan | Price | Reading allowance | Questions | Speech to text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free | EUR 0 / month | base tier | 20 / month | 1 hour / month |
| Writer | EUR 12 / month | 10x the Free tier | 200 / month | 10 hours / month |
| Author | EUR 29 / month | 25x the Free tier | 600 / month | 40 hours / month |

Every paid tier includes the whole writing page, maths and all, plus the threads feature. VAT is added at checkout where it applies. The footnote under the table matters more than it looks: how far the reading goes depends on how long your books are and how much they change, so a 900-page draft and a 40-page story do not consume the same allowance.

Extra usage can be bought once when you run out, and the terms say it is kept until used and is not refunded once used.

## Your writing, your rules

The privacy design is the most careful part of the product, and it works per book rather than per account. Each book carries its own switches:

| Switch | What it controls |
|---|---|
| Foolscap may read this Work | Off makes the book private: nothing is sent to any AI, and dictation stays on Foolscap's own servers |
| Read along while writing | Off means the book is read only when you ask |
| Research in the background | Off means research happens only on request |
| May search the web | Off means nothing is looked up for that book |
| Research may search with the Work's words | Off means web searches use ideas and names only, never your own text |

The policy also answers the things people normally have to ask about. Nobody at Foolscap reads your writing, and it is never used to train any AI model, by Foolscap or by any provider it works with. AI providers receive only the passages a feature needs and the question you asked, never your name or email. Stripe takes the card details on its own page, so payment data never reaches Foolscap. Books sit on an EU hosting provider, and transfers to providers outside the EU use the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.

Everything is kept versioned, and there is a "Download it all" button that hands you every book as plain text with every version in it. Connect your own git host and your books and their versions are pushed to the repository you choose. Backups are encrypted, taken daily, and kept for up to eight weeks, so anything you delete is gone from them within about two months. Passwords are one-way hashes, and access keys to your own repository are sealed and never shown.

Cookies are limited to three: one to keep you signed in, one to take you straight to your writing, and one to remember an invitation for fourteen days. Visit counting is cookieless, and the IP address is turned into a rotating daily code that cannot be reversed.

All three legal pages are plain HTML, so you can read them as text and check the wording yourself before you commit a manuscript to it.

```bash
agent-reach read "https://www.foolscap.works/privacy.html" | jq -r '.items[]?.text // empty'
agent-reach read "https://www.foolscap.works/terms.html" | jq -r '.items[]?.text // empty'
agent-reach read "https://www.foolscap.works/start.html" | jq -r '.items[]?.text // empty'
```

## What to watch for

Some limits are worth stating plainly before you commit a manuscript to it.

The service is proprietary. There is no source repository linked anywhere on the site and no licence to read. The books themselves are yours, and the git history is yours, but the application is not open.

The reading allowance is the soft ceiling of the whole product, and it is measured in a unit the vendor admits is fuzzy. If you are writing something long that changes constantly, the Free and Writer tiers will not be enough, and the Author tier at EUR 29 a month is the real price of the tool.

Fair use rules prohibit running Foolscap automatically, reselling it, and using it to build a competing service, with suspension as the sanction. Liability is capped at whatever you paid in the twelve months before a claim. If the service ever shuts down, you get 60 days notice and time to take everything away, which is a promise rather than a contract.

Two smaller things. There is no Lean support, so if your mathematics needs formal verification you are outside the tool's reach for now. And the public surface of the site is four pages: home, plans, terms and privacy. There is no documentation site, no changelog and no public roadmap, so the fastest way to find out what changed is the launch thread.

One honest caveat about this article. I could read the home page, the pricing page, the privacy policy, the terms, the launch thread and the demo video, but the app itself sits behind a sign-in. The account side of the product, the side agent and the speech to text are described here from the vendor's own material and demo, not from a working account.
