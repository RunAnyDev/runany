## Where this category sits

Video restoration covers the work of taking degraded footage and putting it back into a usable state. In practice that umbrella spans a few distinct problems:

- Upscaling footage that was captured at a lower resolution than you need
- Recovering detail that compression or transmission artefacts smoothed away
- Handling per-frame flicker and temporal inconsistency that per-frame processing introduces
- Working with formats that decoders rarely handle well

Tools in this space usually have to deal with that third item, because restoring frames independently is easy and produces a subtle result that people notice immediately. Consistency across neighbouring frames is what separates a restoration that looks acceptable from one that looks like it is boiling.

Python being the language of record for this category is not a coincidence. The tooling ecosystem around neural restoration is Python-first, so a Python tool sits where the models and the converters already are.

## The licensing question

AGPL-3.0 is the part of this repository worth slowing down on. It is the strongest copyleft of the common open source licences, and unlike GPL-3.0 it carries network use into scope. If you run a modified version of jasna as a service and let other people reach it over a network, you are required to offer those people the corresponding source.

That is fine for personal use, for research, and for internal tools. It matters if you were planning to build something commercial on top of it without releasing your changes. Check your own situation before committing to it.
