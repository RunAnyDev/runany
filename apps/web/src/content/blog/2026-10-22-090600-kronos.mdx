> **TL;DR:** Kronos is a foundation model for financial market data, published under MIT by a single maintainer. The repository is compact in scope and has not been pushed to since April 2026, yet it picked up 575 stars on the trending page today.

## What it is

Kronos is described in one line: a foundation model for the language of financial markets. That framing is doing real work. Instead of modeling text, it models market data as a sequence language, where price and volume series get tokenized and predicted much the way sentences do.

The repository is published by `shiyu-coder`, a user-owned account, in Python under the MIT license. It has 42,857 stars, 7,071 forks and 283 open issues, and it was created on 2025-07-01. The default branch is `master`.

MIT matters more here than it does for most trending repositories. If you want to read the implementation, fine-tune it, or ship a commercial product built on top of it, the license lets you do all three without asking.

A market foundation model is the kind of artifact that gets pulled into agent projects, notebooks and derivative research, and each of those downstream uses can push the upstream repository back onto the trending page. The star count is evidence of interest, not evidence of recent development, and it is worth separating those two things when you evaluate the project.

## When to use it

Kronos is a reasonable starting point if you are prototyping a forecaster on market series and want a pretrained backbone rather than a model trained from scratch. The MIT license removes the negotiation step, which matters for a research project that might turn into a product.

## When to skip it

Skip it if you need a production trading signal. This is an academic-scale repository with 283 open issues and no release cadence in six months, which makes it a research dependency rather than a vendor. It also does not handle live data plumbing, execution or risk, so you would be assembling those parts yourself.
