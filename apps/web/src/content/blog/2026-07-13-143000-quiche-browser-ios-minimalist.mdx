## What Is Quiche Browser?

Quiche Browser is an iOS app built by a single independent developer. It launched on Hacker News in early 2026 and garnered 226 points. The pitch is direct: a browser that does not get in your way.

The press has called it "a very robust, exquisitely designed, stunningly handsome web browser" — and noted it is "just so" in its simplicity.

Unlike mainstream browsers that ship with tab managers, sync ecosystems, and extension stores, Quiche strips all of that out. The default experience is a single full-screen page with a minimal chrome. You navigate by swiping, tapping the URL bar to type, and the interface fades when you scroll.

### Core Design Philosophy

The browser is funded directly by its users — not by ads, data harvesting, or venture capital. This is structurally different from most browsers, which are vehicles for ad networks and user tracking at scale.

The implications for the product:
- No incentive to keep you in the app longer than necessary
- No promoted content or "discovery" feeds
- No dark patterns designed to extract permissions

## Features

**Minimal UI by default**
The tab bar is hidden until you need it. The URL bar collapses when you scroll. Reading mode kicks in automatically on article pages, stripping ads and navigation clutter.

**Independent funding**
Quiche is a paid app with no ad network dependency. Updates are funded by the people who use it, not by a surveillance economy.

**iOS-native experience**
Built for iOS from the ground up. It uses the system web view but wraps it in custom UI that feels closer to a native reading app than a desktop browser ported to mobile.

**Privacy-conscious**
No analytics SDK, no crash reporting that phones home, no sync servers that hold your browsing history. Your data stays on your device.

## FAQ

**Q: Is Quiche open source?**
**A:** No. It is a proprietary, single-developer app. There is no public source repository.

**Q: Does it sync tabs across devices?**
**A:** No. There is no sync feature. All browsing data stays on the device where it was created.

**Q: Does it block ads?**
**A:** Quiche does not ship with a built-in ad blocker. iOS Content Blocker extensions can be installed separately if needed.

**Q: How is the developer funded?**
**A:** Through direct purchases on the App Store. There is no ad network or data monetization.

**Q: Can it run extensions like Safari or Chrome?**
**A:** No. Quiche does not support browser extensions.

## Conclusion

Quiche fills a gap that Apple, Google, and Mozilla have largely ignored: a browser that treats minimalism as a feature rather than a limitation. For readers, researchers, and anyone who finds tab bars and notification badges distracting, it is one of the few genuine alternatives on iOS that does not require a learning curve.

The independent funding model is also worth noting. In a market where browsers are primarily vehicles for ad networks, a tool that survives on direct purchases has structurally different incentives. That does not automatically make it better — but it makes it different, and for a specific kind of user, that difference is the entire point.

If you want a clean reading device on iOS without the ecosystem baggage, Quiche is worth the download.
